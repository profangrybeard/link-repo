Greening the Stop

Teams are converging on vegetation at MARTA stations and stops, with community gardens and markets. This page checks the claims against the evidence, lists how such projects have failed elsewhere, and names the Atlanta ideas that already exist on paper or in a drawer. No design here. Every line links to its source.

The starting thought was a spray-on moss that grows on concrete, cools it, makes oxygen and discourages mold. The instinct behind it is right: bare concrete in Atlanta sun is a hazard, and plants change that. The mechanism is wrong. The evidence says shade does the cooling, and living moss on sun-exposed concrete gets hotter than the concrete.

The moss claim, checked

Reduces heat
Not supported
A 2026 TU Delft field study put moss on mortar in direct sun. By day the moss ran 1.5 to 4.1°C hotter than bare mortar, with daily maxima of 58.6 to 59.4°C against 40.2°C for bare mortar. Watering cooled it for 1 to 1.5 hours. Moss reflects less light than concrete (albedo 0.07 against 0.13). The authors say shading may be the most effective fix. Veeger, Ottelé, Jonkers, Sci. Total Environ., 2026
Creates oxygen
Unverified
Mosses photosynthesize only while wet, and exposed cushions dry out within hours of dawn in summer. No study gives an oxygen figure at the scale of a wall or a stop. Zotz et al., New Phytologist, 2000
Discourages mold
Not supported
The antifungal literature is about chemical extracts from moss, not living mats on surfaces. The one controlled study of living moss panels found the moss itself grew fungi under humidity and watering, with the Hypnum panel half dead at seven months and fully dead at ten. Masonry guidance says biological growth retains water and speeds deterioration. Zechmeister, Sustainability, 2023 Prosoco Tech Note 101 Commisso et al., Plants, 2021
Grows on exposed concrete
Partly
Moss colonizes old, carbonated, persistently damp concrete on its own. On new concrete the Delft protocol needed a bioreceptive mix, six weeks of daily watering and three months under 50% shade cloth. The DIY buttermilk slurry has no documented successes. Botany One on Veeger et al., 2026 Energiforsk, 2021 Moss graffiti
Survives Atlanta sun without irrigation
Not supported
No living-moss installation in a hot, sunny climate has been documented surviving unirrigated. CityTree moss filters with automatic irrigation, tanks and sensors died in London and were removed in 2023. Amsterdam measured under 1% particle reduction and returned its eight panels. Cork spent 404,000 euros on five units and removed them in 2025. Waltham Forest Echo, 2023 Binnenlands Bestuur, 2019 Irish Examiner, 2023
What the evidence says cools a stop

Houston measured 17 stops over 13 summer days in 2023. Unshaded stops averaged a wet-bulb globe temperature of 92.5°F. Trees cut it 5.9°F. A glass shelter cut it 5.9°F, an acrylic-walled one 1.6°F, and a dome shelter that was itself unshaded ran 5.2°F hotter than the open air. In Austin, across 2,271 stops, shelters did not protect ridership on hot days; tree canopy did, slightly. Tree shade cuts peak surface temperatures by 11 to 25°C in the EPA compendium. UTHealth Houston, 2025 IJERPH, Austin, 2021 EPA, Reducing Urban Heat Islands

What the Occlusion Map already says

The need is measured7.5% of the city's bus stops had a shelter in 2021, and a city ordinance capped shelters at 300 until that June. MARTA Army found 12% with shelter and 25% without a paved sidewalk. 2026 had 76 days at or above 90°F through Oct 3 against a normal of 44. The 2021 heat mapping found a 14.5 degree spread, hottest in and south of downtown. Canopy fell from 47.9% to 45.7% between 2008 and 2023 and multifamily development is now the leading cause of loss. 423839
The land is sitting thereThe largest flat public land in the neighborhoods that need shade and food is MARTA parking. H.E. Holmes: 17 to 22 acres, 1,436 spaces at 37% use. Kensington: 41 acres at 25% use. Brookhaven: a 6.5-acre lot beside Buford Highway, a transit-oriented development canceled in 2017 and back in feasibility study in 2025. College Park is the exception at 92% use. 221
The stop is where the walk kills59% of the region's pedestrian deaths in 2024 happened within an eighth of a mile of a bus stop. 58% of stops have no crosswalk within 100 feet. A greener stop with no crossing is still the same stop. Allen London was killed waiting at a Candler Road stop in 2024. 86
Nobody owns the stopThe sidewalk is the city's or GDOT's, the pole and the shelter are MARTA's, installed by a contractor under a 2019 program, the tree needs the city and a utility locate, and the adjacent property owner is liable for the sidewalk under city code. The city had no transportation department until 2019. The first question for any planting is who waters it on a Sunday in August. 121
Money exists and goes elsewhereThe city's Tree Trust Fund held about $14 million in 2020; an audit found $2.9 million of $22 million spent on salaries and overhead. The 2025 tree ordinance raised recompense fees. More MARTA money went to capital projects while the Peachtree Street bus was cut. A full shelter costs about $18,500 to $22,000 in Phoenix, Austin and Houston. Saporta Report, 2020 Rough Draft, 2025 SAN, Phoenix 1925

How these projects fail, with numbers

  1. Nobody waters itLiving walls die from irrigation, drainage, design or a budget line. Edmonton removed a living wall in 2020 over more than $70,000 a year in upkeep. Sheffield's 2011 wall died of drainage problems and a brand-new one had brown panels at its unveiling. Atlanta City Hall's 2003 green roof had lapsed by 2017 and needed a $135,000 restoration. Modular living walls run 50 to 150 euros per square meter per year to maintain. Global News, 2020 Sheffield Star, 2024 Saporta Report, 2017 Fassadengrün cost comparison
  2. The volunteer cliffA survey of 445 garden organizations counted 1,615 community gardens lost between 2007 and 2012, for lack of gardener interest, loss of land and loss of funding, in that order. A Florida expert panel ranked lack of committed volunteers first at 76.9%, ahead of land, funding, water and vandalism. Browns Mill Food Forest in Lakewood runs on volunteers and one part-time ranger by design. Drake and Lawson, 2015 Diaz et al., 2018 AJC, Browns Mill
  3. Land tenure, and station land is interim by definitionTruly Living Well lost its Old Fourth Ward lease around 2015 and had to move. Philadelphia has lost more than a fifth of its gardens in 25 years and only 38.3% of garden parcels have secure ownership. New York sent bulldozers in 2000. At Five Points, the station's community garden, soccer field and market all closed in May 2025 when the $230 million rebuild began. Anything on a MARTA lot has to assume eviction. Saporta Report, Truly Living Well Nature of Cities, Philadelphia, 2026 GrowNYC, 1999 auction Atlanta News First, 2025
  4. The soilEmory tested 355 Westside soil samples; most were above the University of Georgia's 75 ppm low-risk lead level and a few above the EPA's 400 ppm. The EPA found 64 elevated sites and expanded to 368 properties. Atlanta garden leaders named potable water access, funding and contaminated compost as barriers. On the west side, growing in the ground is a liability before it is a garden. Emory, 2019 Saikawa Lab focus groups
  5. Market economicsOregon tracked 62 farmers markets: 32 closed, 15 of them after one season and 30 within four years, with manager turnover the marker. Chicago's Fresh Moves produce bus lost $185,520 on sales in 2012 and parked. Washington Metro refused a station market in 2011 because selling food on its property broke its rules. The Fresh MARTA Market has lasted since 2015 with USDA grants ($500,000 in 2017, $851,677 in 2021) and an institutional operator, and it still lost its Five Points site to construction. No sales figures have been published since 2021. Oregon State, 2006 Nonprofit Quarterly, Fresh Moves DCist, 2011 MARTA Market
  6. The shelter that makes it hotterHouston's unshaded dome shelter ran 5.2°F hotter than open air. Austin's new shelters have roofs of about 60 square feet against 100 on the old ones, and riders say there is not enough shade. Los Angeles built La Sombrita, a shade pole under $10,000, because the transportation department has no authority to build shelters; it drew ridicule for shade that does not follow the sun. UTHealth Houston, 2025 KUT, 2023 Streetsblog LA, 2023
  7. The agency will not plant a treeHouston METRO avoids trees at stops because roots damage the bus pad, a third of its stops sit beside drainage ditches, and utilities and right-of-way get in the way; a tree with two years of watering and ten of pruning costs about $400. Miami-Dade's planting rules for stops set clear-sight limits and setbacks and name no maintenance owner. Los Angeles delivered 660 of 2,185 promised ad-funded shelters in about twenty years. MARTA's 2020 sustainability report lists native plantings only as something being explored, and the city's 2017 green infrastructure plan never mentions a bus stop. Texas Standard, 2023 Miami-Dade, Growing Green Bus Stops MARTA sustainability report, 2020 Atlanta GI plan, 2017
  8. Colliding with what is already thereWhen the West End station market launched in 2015 it was scheduled over an existing community garden's hours, and the garden's director called it divisive. Six MARTA Markets, six StationSoccer fields, Truly Living Well, the BeltLine urban farm and Browns Mill already occupy this space. The map of who is already doing it comes before the idea. Atlanta Progressive News, 2015 Urbanize, StationSoccer
  9. The ad contract is the only model that has scaledUtrecht's 316 green-roof shelters exist because an outdoor-advertising contractor installs and maintains them for 15 to 20 years out of ad revenue, and the city pays nothing. Leicester copied it with 30. Philadelphia built one green-roof shelter in 2011, maintained by the builder for two years, and never built a second. Atlanta's own code capped shelters at 300, counting benches, until June 2021. City of Utrecht RCI, Leicester, 2021 Greenroofs.com, 2011 Saporta Report, 2021

What nobody has measured

Each of these is an absence, which is also an opening. A measurement is a deliverable.

  • Any air or surface temperature at an Atlanta bus stop. Houston, Phoenix, Tucson and Austin have numbers. Atlanta has none in public.
  • Whether the North Avenue Arboretum canopy pilot was ever built, and what it cost.
  • MARTA Market sales or visitor counts since 2021, and why the original operator left.
  • How many lots Grows-A-Lot has licensed since 2018.
  • The Tree Trust Fund balance after 2020 and what the 2025 ordinance has collected.
  • MARTA's per-shelter cost under the 2019 Tolar contract.
  • The ownership and maintenance split for a single stop: who holds the sidewalk, the pole, the shelter, the tree pit and the water.
