Atlanta Occlusion Map

Where the last mile breaks inside the Perimeter. documented blockages in the city's walking, rolling and transit capillaries, with the evidence and the history of why each one is still there. No solutions on this page, by design.

If the city is a heart, the interstates and the rail lines are its big vessels and they get the attention. The trip from a bus stop to a front door, from a station to a hospital shift, from an apartment to a grocery store across seven lanes, runs through capillaries. That is where Atlanta's trips fail, and most of those failures are old enough to feel like weather.

This map holds the failures that can be documented: a death with a date, a plan with a year, a vote with a margin. Each site names who it hits and why it persists. The reasons sort into five kinds: the thing is so familiar nobody sees it, it was studied and shelved, an organized neighborhood beat the fix, the road belongs to a government that does not answer to the people walking on it, or the money went elsewhere.

How to read it

Click a numbered site on the map or in the index. The dossier gives the blockage, who it hits, the evidence with a link and data year for every claim, and the reason it persists. Marker size is scale: one documented episode, a neighborhood, or a corridor or decades-long pattern. Filter by kind of blockage or by reason. Claims marked medium rest on a secondary source or older data.

Coordinates are placed at the nearest intersection or landmark. The schematic base is drawn by hand from known interchanges and station locations. Street tiles load where the page is allowed to reach the network.

    What could not be found

    Six research streams ran on Oct 9, 2026 with the rule that every number had to come from a page actually read. These are the numbers that do not exist in public, or could not be reached. Each is a finding: the city does not measure it, or does not publish it.

      Method

      Scope is the area inside I-285: the City of Atlanta plus the inside-Perimeter parts of DeKalb and Fulton. Sites were chosen where a blockage could be tied to a documented death, count, study, ordinance, vote or demolition. Every claim links to its source and states the data year; where sources conflict, the conflict is stated. Claims marked medium rest on a secondary source, older data or a figure the primary document implies rather than states. Nothing here is a recommendation. The page exists to make need visible before anyone proposes anything.
